## How to use these templates

{{productName}} already writes a first email, a DM and a call opener for every lead that scores high enough, and the Follow-up Autopilot drafts your follow-ups. So why templates?

Use them when:

- a draft needs a rewrite, or its **AI Status** column says "Template used" (the backup pitch starts "I was looking at how [Short name] shows up online, and one thing stood out:" and quotes the top evidence);
- a lead is **Pitch ready** (no public email), so you call or send a DM (direct message);
- the owner gives you an email address on the phone;
- you contacted someone by DM or phone, because the Autopilot only follows up emails;
- someone replies. The workflow never answers replies for you.

Copy a template, replace every bracket, read it once out loud, and send it yourself. Nothing here is sent automatically, and nothing should be sent in bulk.

### The placeholders

| Placeholder | What to put there | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| [Business] | The full business name | **Business** column |
| [Short name] | The name without "LLC", "Ltd" or "Pvt Ltd" | "Sunrise Family Dental", not "Sunrise Family Dental, PLLC" |
| [Business type] and [City] | What you searched for, and where | **Search** column, for example "dentists in Austin, TX" |
| [Evidence] | One true fact about their problem, in plain words | **Evidence** column (numbered, top problem first). Each niche below shows how to rewrite it. |
| [Evidence, short] | The same fact in 5-10 words, for DMs, calls and voicemails | For example "your website is very slow on phones" |
| [Your offer] | Your free first step, written to them ("your website", not "their website"). The default offers already do this. | **yourOffer** in *⚙️ Settings* |
| [Your service] | What you sell | **yourService** in *⚙️ Settings* |
| [Your name], [Your business], [Your phone] | You | Your signature |
| [Their name] | The name they sign with when they reply | Their reply. If there is none, write "Hi [Short name] team". |
| [Day and time] | Real times when you are free | Your calendar |

> **Important:** Check the evidence yourself before you send anything: open the website, look at the Google listing, or run the test again. The checks are careful and skip what they can't verify (the **Notes** column often says why), but they can still be wrong. If you can't see the problem, don't mention it. One wrong fact makes the whole message look like spam.

### The ending of every email

The first emails the workflow drafts already end with your **signOff** (`Thanks,` unless you change it in *⚙️ Settings*), your signature and the opt-out line. When you write an email by hand, end it the same way:

```text
Thanks,
[Your name]
[Your business]
[Your website and phone]
[Your postal address]

P.S. If this isn't useful, just reply "no thanks" and I won't email again.
```

Below, this ending is shown as [Signature]. Follow-ups end a little differently: Autopilot follow-up drafts already end with your signature and the **followUpOptOutLine** from the Autopilot's *⚙️ Settings*: "If you'd rather I didn't follow up, just reply and I'll stop." You don't need to add one. In the follow-up templates below, [Signature] means your signature plus that line, instead of the P.S. If you empty **followUpOptOutLine**, write your own opt-out sentence into each follow-up.

In the US, the CAN-SPAM law expects a postal address and a clear way to opt out in every commercial email. **yourAddress** is optional in *⚙️ Settings*, but the results page reminds you while it is empty.

### Five rules that keep these templates working

1. **One problem per message.** Use the top one. The rest can wait for the call.
2. **No links or attachments in the first message.** Spam filters and people distrust them. Links are fine once someone replies.
3. **One yes/no question at the end.**
4. **Be kind about the problem.** Say "this is slowing you down", not "this is terrible".
5. **No made-up numbers or results.** Only quote the sheet or a test you ran yourself.

### Calls, voicemails and DMs

- Call in business hours, but not at their busiest time. Ask for the owner or manager.
- If they give you 30 seconds, say the problem and your offer in one sentence each. Then stop and listen. If they are interested, ask for the best email address and send your offer the same day.
- Leave one voicemail, not five. Speak slowly and say your number twice.
- Send DMs one at a time, by hand, with no links or voice notes. Use WhatsApp only with a number the business shows for customer messages.
- Follow up a call or DM once at most, by hand.

> **Warning:** Calls and messages have their own rules in many countries, for example the TPS and CTPS do-not-call lists in the UK, US rules on auto-dialers and marketing texts, and the DND (Do Not Disturb) register in India. OUTREACH-PLAYBOOK.pdf explains them. Stop as soon as someone asks you to.

### Follow-ups

The Autopilot drafts follow-up #1 in the same Gmail thread 3 or more days after your first email, and follow-up #2 7 or more days after that. Then it stops. Each draft already ends with your signature and the follow-up opt-out line. It never drafts a follow-up once someone has replied or the email bounced. Use the templates to edit its drafts, or to follow up by hand after a DM or call. Each follow-up #1 adds one new angle; each follow-up #2 makes it easy to say no.

## Replies

Answer every reply within one working day. Keep it short and human. Links are fine now, because they wrote to you. Then update the **Status** in your sheet as shown. The Autopilot marks a lead **Replied** when it sees a reply in your pitch's Gmail thread (even from another address, such as a colleague or the owner's personal email; **Notes** then says who replied) or an email from the lead's address. Out-of-office auto-replies don't count. It never follows up a **Replied** lead again. If your email bounced, it sets **Bounced** instead: try a call or DM.

## Video audit script (2 minutes)

When someone says "yes, send it", record and send the video within 24-48 hours, in one take. Any screen recorder works.

| Time | Show | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:15 | Their homepage or Google listing | "Hi [Their name], I'm [Your name]. You said yes to a short video, so here it is. It takes two minutes." |
| 0:15-0:50 | The problem, live | "This is your [website / Google listing] as a new customer sees it. Look at this: [show the problem, don't just describe it]." |
| 0:50-1:15 | Why it matters | "When this happens, [the likely cost in plain words: people leave, call someone else, or can't book]." |
| 1:15-1:45 | What you would fix | "Here's what I would change: [Fix 1], [Fix 2] and [Fix 3]." |
| 1:45-2:00 | One next step | "If this is useful, reply to my email and we can have a 15-minute call. If not, no problem, and thanks for watching." |

What to show live in each niche:

- **Web design:** the site on your phone, and Google's PageSpeed test run live, so the result is not just your opinion. The score changes a few points between runs, so describe it as Google's simulated phone test.
- **Local SEO:** a normal Google search for their business type and area, and what the top results have that they lack.
- **Google reviews & reputation:** their Maps listing next to two nearby businesses, then how simple a review request can be.
- **Social media & ads:** their website with no social links, their Instagram if they have one, and one post you would make.
- **AI chatbots & booking:** trying to book on their website at night, then what an assistant could answer.

> **Tip:** Close other tabs and turn off notifications before you record, and never show other clients' data. Upload the video as *unlisted* (only people with the link can watch it) and send the link. Describe what you would do; don't promise results.

## Discovery call agenda

A *discovery call* is a short call to understand their business before you suggest anything. Plan 20 minutes. Before it, reread their row in the sheet, open their website and Google listing, and have your price range ready.

| Minutes | Part | What to say or ask |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Open | "Thanks for making time. My plan: I'll ask a few questions, then share what I'd suggest. Is 20 minutes still OK?" |
| 2-7 | Their business | "Who are your best customers?" "Where do most new customers come from today?" |
| 7-12 | The problem | Show the evidence again. "How does this affect you day to day?" Let them describe the cost in their own words. |
| 12-15 | What they tried | "Have you worked on this before? What happened?" |
| 15-18 | Budget, timing, decision | "Do you have a budget in mind?" "When would you like this done?" "Does anyone else decide with you?" |
| 18-20 | Next step | "I'll send a one-page proposal by [Day]. Can we talk on [Day] to go through it?" |

Add one question for your niche:

| Niche | Extra question |
|---|---|
| Web design | "When people visit the website, what do you want them to do: call, book or visit?" |
| Local SEO | "Which searches do you most want to show up for?" |
| Google reviews & reputation | "How do you ask customers for feedback today?" |
| Social media & ads | "Who posts for you now, and how often?" |
| AI chatbots & booking | "What are the five questions your team answers most often?" |

Listen more than you talk. Write short notes in the **Notes** column straight after the call.

## Post-call follow-up

Send this on the day of the call. It shows you listened and lets them correct you before you write the proposal.

```text
Subject: Notes from our call

Hi [Their name],

Thanks for your time today. Here's what I heard, so we're on the same page:

- Your goal: [their goal, in their words]
- The main problem: [the evidence, plus what they said it costs them]
- What matters most to you: [timing, budget or another priority]

As promised, I'll send a one-page proposal by [Day]. If I got anything wrong, just reply and tell me.

[Signature]
```

## Proposal email

Send it within 48 hours of the call. Keep the email short; the details go in the one-page proposal.

```text
Subject: Proposal for [Short name]

Hi [Their name],

As promised, here's my proposal for [Short name]: [Link or attached PDF]

In short:
- Your goal: [their goal]
- What I'll do: [the main work, in one line]
- Timeline: start [Date], finished by [Date]
- Price: [Option 1 and price], [Option 2 and price] or [Option 3 and price]

To start, I'll need [logo, photos, logins or other items]. To go ahead, just reply "yes" with the option you prefer, and I'll send [the agreement / the deposit invoice].

Is there anything you'd like me to change before you decide?

[Signature]
```

### One follow-up after the proposal

No answer after 2-3 days? Send one short note, then wait.

```text
Hi [Their name],

Did you get a chance to look at the proposal? I'm happy to answer questions or adjust the scope to fit your budget. If now isn't the right time, just tell me and I'll close the file.

[Signature]
```

Then set **Status** to **Won** for a yes, or **Lost** for a no or two weeks of silence.

## Phrases to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| "I hope this finds you well" | Filler that every cold email uses | Start with the fact |
| "I came across your business" | Vague: it could be sent to anyone | Say exactly where you looked: "on Google Maps" |
| "Loved your work!" or "Big fan" | Fake familiarity: they know you are a stranger | Say honestly how you found them |
| "Most businesses lose half their customers..." | An invented statistic | Only numbers from the sheet or a test you ran |
| "Only two spots left this month" | Pressure | No deadlines; let them decide |
| "Just following up" or "Bumping this" | Adds nothing | Add one new, useful angle |
| "Guaranteed first page" or "Double your bookings" | A promise you can't keep | Describe what you will do, not results |

These templates help you write honest, specific messages. They can't guarantee replies or clients. Results depend on your niche, area, offer and care.
